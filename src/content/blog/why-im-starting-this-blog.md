I’ve sat down to write this post more times than I’d like to admit. Every time, I got a few sentences in and closed the tab, not sure I had anything worth saying. What finally changed my mind wasn’t a big revelation — it was just getting tired of feeling like I had to have it all figured out before I was allowed to talk about it.

So here’s the honest version: MS is part of my life now, and I’m still working out what that means day to day. That includes the physical side, but also the mental and cognitive realities — the brain fog, the shifting focus, and the quiet internal adjustments that don't always show on the outside.

At the same time, my perspective is shaped by Schizoid Personality Disorder (SPD). Living with SPD means I naturally process the world through a lens of independence, solitude, and emotional containment. When you combine a condition like MS with a personality structured around self-reliance and low social drive, it creates a very specific way of moving through illness, healthcare, and daily life.

Some of this I’ve made peace with. Some of it I’m still navigating. Most of it falls somewhere in between — a slow, ongoing process of learning what works, what doesn’t, and how to keep showing up for my life anyway.

**What to expect here**

I'm not planning to turn this into a highlight reel, and I'm also not planning to turn it into a
symptom log nobody wants to read. Somewhere in between: real updates, things I'm learning,
strategies that are working (and ones that flopped), and probably some posts that are just about
having a bad day and needing to say so.

I don't know exactly what shape this blog will take yet — I think that's something I'll figure
out by actually writing it, post by post. If you're here, thank you for reading along while I do.

— Chris
